## PART 1 — POSITIONING FOUNDATION

### Unique Selling Proposition

**Primary USP:**
> I design the brand, build the product, and teach you to run it — so you're not dependent on me afterwards.

**Why this works:**
- It's *provable* (you actually do all three, unlike most claims)
- It's *specific* (three named deliverables, not "digital systems")
- It's *counter-intuitive* (freelancers don't usually promise independence — memorable)
- It maps directly to your Brand Promise doc

**Supporting proof points** (use these as sub-lines, never all at once):
1. Most designers stop at visuals. Most developers stop at function. I do both, then explain why.
2. Every site is custom-coded. No WordPress, no page builders.
3. Design decisions come from your story and context — not from a trend list.
4. You leave knowing how your system works, not just owning files.

### Positioning Statement (site-wide anchor)
> I help individuals and businesses build intuitive brands, websites, and mobile apps that feel personal, professional, and consistent — while teaching them how to sustain and grow their own systems.

### Who it's for (say this out loud on the site)
- Founders and small business owners who need a brand + site that actually work together
- Students and early-career designers/developers who want mentorship through real projects
- Practicing creatives who are strong in one skill and want to bridge design ↔ development

### Who it's NOT for (state 2–3 publicly — it builds trust)
- People who want a fast, cheap, template-based site
- Anyone needing WordPress or page-builder work
- Rushed, last-minute projects

## PART 2 — SITE ARCHITECTURE

```
/                       Home (one-page sell)
/projects               Work index (all case studies)
/projects/[slug]        Individual case study  ← the highest-value pages
/about                  Full story, photo, credentials, personal context
/services               Detailed offer + process + what working with you is like
/contact                Form + direct email + socials
/blog (Phase 2)         Teaching content — do NOT build until you have 3 posts
```

**Navigation:** Work · About · Services · Contact
(Put **Work** first. It's what people came for.)

**Why hybrid, not one-page:**
Research consensus for 2026 is that reviewers scan the homepage in 30–60 seconds, then spend 5–10 minutes on your first case study. A one-page site gives them nowhere to spend those 10 minutes. The scroll page sells; the case pages convince.

## PART 4 — CASE STUDY PAGE TEMPLATE

Every case study uses this exact structure. Consistency lets people compare projects fast.

```
1. HERO
   — Project name
   — One-line outcome (what CHANGED, not what you built)
   — Meta row: Role · Timeline · Tools · Client (or "Self-initiated")
   — Full-bleed hero image

2. THE SETUP  (2–3 sentences)
   — What was the product?
   — What was broken?
   — Why did it matter?
   Open with the tension. Never open with a company description.

3. MY ROLE  (1 short paragraph or bullet list)
   — Be exact about what you did vs. what a collaborator did.
   — Honesty here is checked in interviews.

4. RESEARCH & CONSTRAINTS
   — What you learned before designing
   — Budget, timeline, technical, or cultural constraints
   — Even informal research counts: 3 user conversations is research.

5. KEY DECISIONS  ← the section that actually gets you hired
   — 3–4 decisions, each with: what you chose, what you rejected, why
   — Per your own Portfolio Tips: explain WHY this typeface, WHY this color
   — Show a wrong turn. "We tried X, it failed, here's what we learned" is
     more credible than a flawless narrative.

6. THE SOLUTION
   — Final screens in quality mockups, varied environments
   — Real content, never lorem ipsum
   — Annotate: callouts explaining what each screen solves

7. OUTCOMES
   — Numbers if you have them
   — Qualitative if you don't: user feedback, client quote, what shipped,
     what got easier. "The client now updates the site themselves" is a
     real outcome.

8. WHAT I'D DO DIFFERENTLY  (2–3 sentences)
   — Optional but powerful. Self-awareness reads as seniority.

9. NEXT PROJECT
   — Auto-link to the next case study so nobody dead-ends.
```

**Length:** 800–1,500 words. Scannable — strong headings, short sections, images breaking up text.

## PART 5 — ABOUT PAGE

Not a longer version of the snapshot. A different job: make people *trust and like* you.

1. **Photo** — good quality, natural, not a stiff headshot
2. **Opening hook** — how you got into design/code. Real story, real specifics.

4. **What I reject** — 2–3 items. Publicly rejecting things is rare and memorable. Use: design without purpose, trend-chasing, gatekeeping knowledge.
5. **Beyond work** — 2 sentences of something human. This is what makes people email you.
6. **Where to find me** — socials, handle, email

## PART 7 — TECHNICAL & UX REQUIREMENTS

| Area | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Mobile** | Test every page on a real phone. Your Social-First Rule applies to the site. |
| **Speed** | Under 3s load. Compress every image. Lazy-load below the fold. |
| **Accessibility** | WCAG AA contrast, alt text on all images, keyboard nav, visible focus states |
| **SEO** | Unique title + meta description per page. Your name in the homepage title: "Lespa — Brand & Product Designer, Cameroon" |
| **Animation** | Subtle only. Scroll reveals, hover states, page transitions. Nothing that delays reading. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. |
| **Contact form** | Must actually send. Test it. Add a visible success state. |
| **Analytics** | Install something (Vercel Analytics is free) — you can't iterate on feedback you don't measure. |
| **Domain** | Move off `lespa.vercel.app` to a real domain. `.vercel.app` reads as unfinished. |
| **Design tokens** | Everything from CORE VISUAL.md. No hex in JSX (already in your build prompt). |
